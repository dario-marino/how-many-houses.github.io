How Many Houses?

A neighborhood-level companion to "Building Houses, Yes, But How Many?" — New York City & the Bay Area, by PUMA

Methodology, in brief: for each PUMA (Census Public Use Microdata Area — the finest geography at which household income and rent data are publicly available, roughly the size of a community district), we start from current median rent or home value and current total housing units (ACS 5-year estimates, 2019–2023). A constant-elasticity demand curve is fit through that single observed point using an adjustable elasticity parameter (−1.5 to −0.5, spanning estimates in the literature). Section 1 asks how many additional units would be needed to reach a price you choose. Section 2 asks how many units are needed today to bring each PUMA to a healthy (5–12%, adjustable) gross vacancy rate, and what price would result. Six high-cost Bay Area PUMAs (parts of San Mateo and Santa Clara counties) have Census-topcoded home values at $2,000,000—treat those estimates as a lower bound. This tool does not model cross-neighborhood substitution (e.g. a renter priced out of one area moving to an adjacent one); each PUMA is evaluated independently.
Data: U.S. Census Bureau ACS 5-Year Estimates & TIGER/Line PUMA boundaries.
