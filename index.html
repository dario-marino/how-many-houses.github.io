How Many Houses?

A neighborhood-level companion to "Building Houses, Yes, But How Many?" — New York City & the Bay Area, by ZIP code

Methodology, in brief: for each ZIP code (ZCTA) in New York City and the 9-county Bay Area, we start from a current market price and current total housing units, then fit a constant-elasticity demand curve through that single observed point using an adjustable elasticity parameter (−1.5 to −0.5, spanning estimates in the literature). Set a target price and the tool shows how many additional units each ZIP would need to reach it, at your chosen elasticity.

Data sources: price comes from Zillow's ZHVI (home value) and ZORI (rent) indices, both updated monthly and based on current listings/asking prices — not a blend with older, long-standing leases. Housing unit counts come from the U.S. Census Bureau's ACS 5-year estimates (2019–2023), the most current source for unit counts at this geography; unit counts move slowly, so this lag matters less than it would for price. A small number of low-sample ZIP codes are suppressed by Zillow and will show as "n/a."

This tool does not model cross-neighborhood substitution (e.g. a renter priced out of one ZIP moving to an adjacent one); each ZIP is evaluated independently.
Data: Zillow Research (ZHVI, ZORI) & U.S. Census Bureau ACS 5-Year Estimates and TIGER/Line ZCTA boundaries.
